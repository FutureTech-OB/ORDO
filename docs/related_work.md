## Human-choice fidelity and decision-model efficiency — 2026-10-17

Contribution two distinguishes predicting actual human choices from solving decision tasks optimally. [Pilli and Nallur (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11049v2), *Predicting Biased Human Decision-Making with Large Language Models in Conversational Settings*, reports a preregistered study of 1,648 participants across six tasks. In those settings, the tested GPT-4 family aligned with human biases better than GPT-5 and the tested open models. This supports treating general model capability and behavioral fidelity as separate targets; it does not isolate reasoning as the cause or establish decision-model superiority.

[General Decision Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03935v2) supplies evidence for efficient decision-oriented inference while documenting probability and aggregate-estimation limitations. ORDO proposes a better combination of behavioral fit, latency and cost through decision-oriented interfaces, human-data adaptation and native batching. Decision models may themselves be built from LLMs. Shorter inference alone does not reproduce bounded rationality, and Jev is not assumed to be inherently human-like. The Centaur evidence below motivates adaptation to human choices, subject to provider and training access.

The official repository distinguishes legacy v1 urban simulation from the recommended v2 research platform. The [v1 UI guide](https://agentsociety.readthedocs.io/en/latest/01-get-started/04-webui.html) separates an Agent/Block behavior template from an agent profile and combines them into experiment agents. Recommended path: retain the working Mesa adapter, borrow the separation of modules/profiles/configuration and expose template discovery next. To integrate an upstream environment later, explicitly map its observation, available choices, action submission and settlement timing into the decision SDK, with parity checks against that environment's rules. This is a proposed adapter, not existing interoperability. Public goods is a useful interactive starting point; adding departments, manager allocation or accountability requires new mechanisms and cannot be claimed from its existing game example alone.

AS2 already supports research orchestration, modular environments, replay and parallel execution. We should not claim these as firsts or imply that it cannot express organization structures. Its documented execution batches are scheduler-level groups; the specific comparison we need is matched ABM/environment behavior, fresh decision requests, native Jev batching, total cost, latency and resulting organizational dynamics. No such comparative benchmark was run in this review. Broad superiority claims remain unsupported.

### Centaur and the human-data feedback loop — 2026-10-17

The Nature Centaur study supplies evidence that human-choice fine-tuning can improve held-out behavioral prediction, including evaluated task-transfer settings. See the [published paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09215-4) and [open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12390832/).

ORDO plans a human-data feedback loop: align observations with decision contexts, calibrate populations or behavior, evaluate held-out data and identify further evidence. Compare random and feedback-guided data additions at equal budgets. Fixed APIs and trainable engines use distinct adaptation routes. See the [roadmap](foundation_architecture.md#human-data-feedback-and-reusable-research-accumulation).

### Proposed protocol

1. **Recover the task before comparing behavior.** Implement one published environment with its original action set, payoffs, observation limits, opponent treatment and update order. Preserve a protocol-matched run separately from organizational extensions. Turning an open-ended solution task into a fixed menu changes the task and must be identified as an adaptation.
2. **Predict held-out human actions.** Where trial-level data exist, provide only the history and information available before a human choice. Compare negative log-likelihood and Brier score for genuine action probabilities; accuracy is secondary. Split by participant or interacting group/session, rather than randomly separating dependent rounds. Only measured participant attributes may be used as matched Persona inputs. Baselines without probabilities can enter trajectory comparisons without inventing confidence values.
3. **Generate interaction trajectories.** Feed simulated actions back into the environment. Compare cooperation/switching/coordination trajectories, dispersion and outcomes against the corresponding human condition. For human–model data, preserve the opponent policy and information treatment; replacing the human with an agent is a new evaluation, not evidence already supplied by the original paper. Replaying recorded opponent actions supports conditional prediction but cannot establish counterfactual interactive fidelity.
4. **Recover experimental contrasts.** Compare treatment-effect sizes, time courses and distributions, not only final averages or significance signs. For organizational networks, proposed targets are information coverage, redundant search, solution diversity and performance. Resample independent groups/sessions for uncertainty; simulated rounds are not independent human observations. If only published summaries are available, restrict the assessment to those summaries.
5. **Test transfer, then scale.** Freeze any prompt selection or probability calibration on development data before testing held-out conditions. Record unknown training-data overlap for hosted models. Extend group size and organizational permissions only after the reference case is assessed; these extensions are predictions to investigate, not empirically validated large organizations.

The matched comparison is task-appropriate rule models, Jev, and one ordinary or thinking LLM where feasible. All receive equivalent task information and legal actions. Report behavioral error alongside actual cost, wall time and native batch throughput. A useful outcome can be comparable behavioral fidelity at lower cost, or a clearly mapped failure boundary; universal Jev superiority is not required for an infrastructure contribution. Native batching may combine simultaneous independent choices or independent worlds, but must not erase within-world sequential dependencies. No cross-agent probability reuse is proposed.

For Stage 1, prioritize a complete matched comparison in one existing ABM or agent-society task. Reuse its empirical reference where available. The human-data procedures above are conditional on that task's data, not a separate required experimental program. This section is a proposed validation plan; no new data extraction, fitting or paid inference was performed for this literature review.

## Existing agent-society comparisons — 2026-10-16

Primary experiment sections were inspected. Existing platforms already compare simulated behavior with empirical data, alternative generators, and component ablations. Our contribution cannot be the mere introduction of such comparisons.

| Platform/source | Verified comparison | Implication for our design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AgentSociety, [ACL Industry 2025 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-industry.94.pdf), Section 4.2/Table 2 | Uses trajectories/intention data from 169 Beijing residents; compares a modeled environment with a textual LLM environment and five classical/generative mobility models. Reported radius divergence is 0.023 with environment vs 0.427 without. | Precedent for a matched environment ablation plus empirical/baseline comparison. It does not establish general superiority over every traditional ABM. |
| OASIS, [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.11581), Sections 3.2–3.3 and Appendix C | Compares 198 real diffusion instances using scale/depth/breadth trajectories and normalized RMSE (reported around 30%); compares initial upvote/downvote/control treatments with human herd-effect results. Simulated propagation is shallower and negative-rating herding is stronger. Also ablates backend models, recommendations and activity timing. | Reuse a published task and metrics, including documented discrepancies. Its million-agent demonstration is distinct from its empirical validation samples. |
| AgentSociety 2, [2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11895), Section 7.2 | Reproduces a public-goods protocol with 24 agents in six groups and ten contribution rounds. Contributions stay near eight tokens instead of showing the human study's sustained cooperation decline. | Particularly relevant organizational-interaction benchmark; assess whether a replacement decision model changes this discrepancy under matched conditions. Specific protocol/code parity remains to be checked. |
| AgentSociety 2, same preprint, Section 7.6/Table 9 | DailyMobility comparison against five generators reports strong intention-sequence alignment but substantially poorer spatial metrics than Act2Loc. | Model realism is dimension-specific. Report successes and failure boundaries rather than one unqualified human-likeness score. |

Recommended sequence: recover the original traditional ABM baseline in the existing NK reference scene; compare Jev under the same environment; then assess portability through one prior agent-society task. Use the original task's outcomes and empirical metrics wherever available, adding native-batch throughput and cost. An engine comparison in a shared environment identifies a different question from an end-to-end platform comparison; both must disclose their configurations. Organizational permissions and authority are extensions after the reference comparison, not replacements for it.

## Expected Parrot product and open-source reference — 2026-10-17

For research applications and proposed ORDO workflows, see the [architecture roadmap](foundation_architecture.md). The related-work review distinguishes founder-affiliated research, the company pilot and third-party EDSL use.

Reviewed official product pages, documentation and the public EDSL repository. This was a documentation/product-architecture review, not an installed-package benchmark or an authenticated UI trial. No upstream code was copied, no account was created, and no remote research data were submitted.

The [EDSL repository](https://github.com/expectedparrot/edsl) publishes a Python research library under the [MIT License](https://github.com/expectedparrot/edsl/blob/main/LICENSE). Its core abstractions include questions/surveys, agents, scenarios, models and results. It supports local or hosted execution and users' own model keys. Reusing specific MIT-covered code requires retaining the stated notices. This does not establish that the complete hosted frontend, Research Agent or service backend is available for self-hosting under the same license.

The [Survey Builder](https://www.expectedparrot.com/products/polly) documents editable study construction and simulation settings. The [Research Agent workflow](https://www.expectedparrot.com/products/research-agent/use-cases) connects study design, execution, analysis and validation. These are useful references for making a research framework approachable through both structured editing and an assistant. EDSL also supports experiments and interaction workflows; avoid describing it as incapable of agent interaction.

Proposed ORDO mappings, not implemented integrations:

| Product pattern to borrow | ORDO interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Reusable typed research objects | Population, organization definition, environment configuration, policy, decision-provider configuration, experiment plan and results |
| Structured study builder | Organization/role/permission editor plus task, market and policy configuration; a shared schema for the browser, CLI and Harness |
| Research assistant operates explicit studies | Harness produces a reviewable plan, invokes registered tools and reports saved results; simulation outcomes come from the executable ABM |
| Provider-independent execution and own keys | Independently configure the Harness model and simulated-agent decision engine; keep local rules usable without provider accounts |
| Saved studies and inspectable results | Export an experiment bundle containing configuration, population snapshot/source references, code/provider versions, seeds, event records and analysis; exclude credentials |
| Templates and sharing | Locally reusable organization and experiment templates first; publish examples and an optional shared catalog later |

The [official documentation](https://docs.expectedparrot.com/en/latest) describes cached response retrieval for exact reproduction. For ORDO, distinguish replaying one recorded realization from conducting an independent new simulation: replay may use its saved probabilities and event draws; new runs continue fresh per-agent/event judgments and declared random seeds. Do not import cross-agent probability reuse as an optimization. Preserve native Jev batching within causal stages.

Recommended architecture: keep Mesa and ORDO's state-transition interfaces as the simulation core; place a consistent experiment service and open-source workbench above them. Treat EDSL as an optional future survey/measurement or validation adapter where a research question needs it. It has not been selected as a required runtime dependency, and no technical compatibility claim has been tested.

The intended product contribution is a reusable organizational ABM workflow linking nested organizations, local information, authority, executable actions and inter-organization outcomes. The strength of this research contribution must be established by matched baseline experiments; an open-source license, assistant or frontend alone is not evidence of methodological novelty or human fidelity.

See the [architecture roadmap](foundation_architecture.md#human-data-feedback-and-reusable-research-accumulation) for the workbench scope. Source is published in the ORDO repository.
